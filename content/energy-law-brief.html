Lead story · Kenya

Paying more at the pump, but less tax on it

Drivers in Nairobi have been paying more since Thursday. EPRA's new prices, which run to 14 November, put super petrol up KSh 12.93 to KSh 226.96 a litre and diesel up KSh 9.90 to KSh 227.76. Kerosene took the biggest jump, up KSh 28.49. EPRA says September's fuel cargoes simply cost more to land.

Pump prices got a reprieve two days later, when the President signed the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026, which keeps VAT on petrol, diesel and kerosene at 8% instead of 16% for another 90 days. The Treasury can now stretch that by a further 90 days with a Gazette notice.

Without the VAT cut, this month's rise would have been a good deal steeper. The next extension sits with the Treasury rather than Parliament, so watch the Gazette as the 90 days run down. The next price review is on 14 November.

Court · Downstream • Medium

Lamu refinery gets the go-ahead, for now

A group of 133 Lamu residents asked the court in Malindi to stop work on Dangote's planned 700,000 barrel-a-day refinery. The judge declined, because the residents had not served the other side and had named the wrong piece of land. They now have a week to fix their petition and two weeks to serve it.

Construction can carry on in the meantime. The bigger questions about the land, the environmental assessment and whether the community was properly consulted have not been decided yet. The case is back in court on 24 November.

Proclamation · Electricity • Medium

About USD 200m for Addis Ababa's grid

Parliament voted unanimously to ratify a World Bank (IDA) loan of SDR 145.8 million, roughly USD 200 million, for the first phase of the PRIME power sector project. The money will pay for new and upgraded substations and power lines in the capital.

Ratification is the step that turns the loan agreement into law, so the money can start to flow. Addis Ababa's demand for power is growing fast, and this work is meant to cut outages and connection delays.

Bill · Renewables & Off-grid • Medium

A regulator just for renewables?

The House of Representatives has backed, at second reading, a bill to amend the Electricity Act 2023 and create a National Authority for Renewable Energy. The new body would take over renewable work now done by NERC, REA and the Rural Electrification Fund, and would get 40% of the Fund's money each year. The bill now goes to committee.

If it passes, mini-grid and off-grid developers would answer to a different regulator for their licences, standards and tariffs. It is early days, but worth following if you work in that space.

Court · Downstream • Medium

Fuel importers win in court; Dangote wants to appeal

The Federal High Court in Abuja ruled that NMDPRA broke the Petroleum Industry Act when it refused petrol import licences to Matrix Energy, AA Nano and AYM Shafa, and ordered it to keep issuing them. Dangote Refinery, which was not a party to the case, has asked the Court of Appeal for permission to appeal.

At stake is whether the regulator can limit fuel imports to protect local refining. The Court of Appeal's answer will matter well beyond these three companies.

Consultation · Retail supply • High

Ofgem considers switching on the Market Stabilisation Charge

Ofgem brought this charge back earlier in the year but left it switched off. Ofgem is considering turning it on. If wholesale prices fall sharply, a supplier that wins a price-cap customer would pay the supplier that lost them, to cover some of the energy already bought for that customer.

It changes the sums on switching and on fixed-price deals, so suppliers and price comparison sites will want a say. Responses are due by 29 October, which leaves only about two weeks.

Consultation · Carbon capture • Medium

A safety valve for carbon capture licences

Ofgem wants to give the two carbon capture network operators, Net Zero North Sea Storage and Liverpool Bay CCS, a way to reopen their allowances if new health and safety rules force big changes to how they build or run their networks.

These are the first two licences of their kind, so the terms set here will shape later ones. Responses are due by 6 November.

Consultation · Heat • Medium

New heat pump targets for boiler makers

The government is asking what the heat pump sales targets should be for years three to six of the Clean Heat Market Mechanism, and how to support the supply chain.

The targets decide how many heat pumps boiler manufacturers must sell, and what they pay if they fall short. Responses are due by 8 November.

Consultation · Upstream • Medium

Taking down North Sea platforms: new guidance

The offshore decommissioning regulator, OPRED, has published a revised draft of its guidance on decommissioning oil and gas installations and pipelines, building on last year's version.

More North Sea assets are reaching the end of their lives, and this guidance shapes how operators plan for that, including when a structure can be left where it is. Responses are due by 6 December.

Minded-to decision · Network charging • Medium

No change likely on standing charges for low-use sites

Some businesses have large grid connections they rarely use, and a proposal known as DCP 412 would move them into a cheaper standing charge band. Ofgem is minded to decline.

If Ofgem confirms that, these sites keep paying what they pay now. If it changes its mind, the cost would be spread across everyone else. Responses are due by 11 December.

Consultation · Data • Medium

Your energy data, shared on your terms

Using powers in the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025, the government wants to make energy companies share a customer's data securely with approved apps and services when the customer asks, much as open banking works today.

Suppliers and other data holders would pick up new legal duties, and new services could be built on top. Responses are due by 8 January 2027.

Policy · Downstream • Medium

Heating oil is finally getting a regulator

Responding to the competition regulator's review of the market, the government has agreed in principle that heating oil distributors should be licensed: by Ofgem in Great Britain and by the Utility Regulator in Northern Ireland, with a compulsory code of conduct. A price cap is off the table for now.

Until now nobody has regulated this market, so households that rely on heating oil have had little protection. New legislation will be needed, and no dates have been set.

Statutory instrument · Offshore wind • Low

A small fix to the Five Estuaries consent

The Five Estuaries Offshore Wind Farm (Correction) Order 2026, SI 2026/1070, tidies up errors in the wind farm's 2025 development consent order. It does not change any policy.

Only relevant if you work with the consent itself, but make sure you are reading the corrected text.
